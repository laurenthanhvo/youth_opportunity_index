YOI Methodology

Youth Opportunity Index methodology

This page documents how the Youth Opportunity Index (YOI) is constructed in the current dashboard build. The dashboard is a working prototype for exploring youth opportunity patterns across San Diego County. It combines tract-level indicators, aggregated geographies, service-access layers, and Workforce-report validation context. The methodology is showcase-ready, but the indicator list and validation layer are still being refined.

Methodology status

Current status
The dashboard should be interpreted as a working decision-support prototype, not as an official final index. The seven-domain YOI structure is stable enough for demonstration, but the exact indicator list, service taxonomy, and Workforce-aligned validation layers are still being improved.
What is stable
The dashboard currently supports multiple comparison geographies, domain-level scoring, custom domain weights, location details, service overlays, Child Opportunity Index comparison, and countywide Workforce-report context indicators.
What is still being refined
The main areas still under refinement are the Youth Supports service taxonomy, exact Workforce-report regional validation metrics, and the distinction between the broad YOI domain scores and report-specific benchmark layers such as the Workforce-aligned education layer.

How the current YOI is built

Base geography
Census tract is the primary analysis geography. The main tract output is data/processed/yoi/yoi_components.csv, which is joined to data/processed/boundaries/sd_tracts.geojson.
Excluded water tracts
Water or harbor tracts are removed from the dashboard so that ocean or harbor areas do not appear as youth-opportunity geographies. The current excluded tract GEOIDs are 06073009902, 06073990100, and 06073990200.
Normalization
Each raw indicator is converted into a county-relative percentile score within San Diego County. Indicators where lower raw values indicate better conditions are reverse-coded so that higher normalized values always represent higher opportunity.
Domain scoring
Indicators are grouped into seven domains: Economic, Education, Health, Housing, Safety / Environment, Mobility / Connectivity, and Youth Supports. Within each domain, available normalized indicators are averaged to create a domain score.
Overall YOI
The current build uses equal domain weights across the seven domains by default. The overall score is stored as a 0–1 value and displayed in the dashboard on a 0–100 scale as yoi_custom_0_100. Users can change the domain weights interactively in the dashboard to explore how different priorities affect the map.
Indicator metadata
Indicator descriptions, source labels, domains, and notes are stored in data/processed/yoi/yoi_indicator_meta.csv. These metadata support the frontend explanation panels and methodology documentation.

Geographic levels shown in the dashboard

The tract-level YOI is the base layer. Other geographies are aggregated from tract-level results so that users can compare opportunity patterns at neighborhood, regional, and policy-relevant scales.

Census tracts
Primary analysis geography. Used for the most detailed map view, location details, and tract-level domain profiles.
ZIP codes
ZIP-level results are aggregated from tract-level YOI outputs and displayed as an alternative comparison geography.
County regions
County-region results summarize the four major regions used in the Workforce report: Metro San Diego, North County, South San Diego, and East San Diego. This level is especially useful for comparing dashboard patterns against the Workforce report.
Supervisor districts
Supervisor district results aggregate tract-level scores into County of San Diego policy districts.
City council districts
City council district results aggregate tract-level scores into San Diego City Council districts.

Current dashboard domains

The seven domains are stable for the current showcase build, but the exact indicator list should still be treated as under review. The dashboard is designed to support exploration and discussion, not to declare a permanent official scoring formula.

Economic
Captures economic stability and labor-market-related conditions using ACS-based indicators such as poverty, median household income, unemployment, and public assistance or SNAP-related measures.
Education
Captures education opportunity using ACS-based indicators such as educational attainment, school enrollment, and youth disconnection proxies. This is a broad YOI domain and is not identical to the Workforce report’s graduation/dropout table.
Health
Captures health-related opportunity using indicators such as uninsured rate, disability rate, frequent mental distress, and poor physical health.
Housing
Captures housing stability and housing conditions using indicators such as rent burden, overcrowding, residential mobility, and homeownership.
Safety / Environment
Captures safety and environmental conditions using indicators such as crime, vacancy, family structure proxies, and environmental burden.
Mobility / Connectivity
Captures transportation and digital connectivity using indicators such as no-vehicle access, transportation barriers, commute-related proxies, and internet subscription proxies.
Youth Supports
Captures access to youth-supporting services and programs. This domain currently includes service-access indicators derived from the local service inventory. This is the domain most actively under review because services can be classified in multiple ways.

Education domain and Workforce-aligned education layer

The dashboard now distinguishes between the broad YOI Education domain and a separate Education — Workforce aligned validation layer.

YOI Education domain
The standard Education domain is part of the overall YOI score. It uses broad ACS-based education opportunity indicators and is available across the dashboard geographies.
Education — Workforce aligned
This is a validation layer, not part of the default YOI score. It is based on the Workforce report Figure 10 education outcomes: high school graduation rate and high school dropout rate. The score combines normalized graduation rate with inverse normalized dropout rate.
Purpose
This layer helps compare the dashboard more directly against the Workforce report without forcing the broad YOI Education domain to match only graduation and dropout. It is shown at the county-region level because Figure 10 is reported for Metro, North, South, and East San Diego.
Interpretation
A higher Workforce-aligned education score means a stronger graduation/dropout profile according to the Figure 10 benchmark. It should be used for validation and explanation, not as a replacement for the broader YOI Education domain unless the methodology is intentionally changed in a future version.

Youth Supports and service methodology

Service locations are used in two ways: they appear as a visible map overlay, and service-access indicators contribute to the Youth Supports domain. Because services can be categorized differently depending on the taxonomy, this part of the methodology is still under review.

Service overlay
The service overlay displays youth-serving locations from the local service inventory. It helps users see where services are located relative to opportunity gaps.
Service-access scoring
The Youth Supports domain includes service-access indicators based on service type, distance, and availability. These indicators are intended to estimate access to support infrastructure, not program quality, capacity, or outcomes.
Current service categories
The current service-access logic includes youth development, barrier reduction, transition pathways, segment-specific supports, and related support categories. Some categories, especially employment and transition-pathway services, may overlap conceptually with the Economic domain, so their final placement should be reviewed.
Current limitation
The Workforce report uses a defined service taxonomy, while the dashboard service layer uses a local service inventory and classification logic. Therefore, service-count comparisons should be interpreted directionally, especially for employment-related services.
Next step
The next methodology improvement is to audit the service taxonomy and decide whether transition-pathway and employment-related services should remain in Youth Supports, move into Economic, or be shown as a separate Workforce-aligned service validation layer.

External reference layer: Child Opportunity Index

What it is
The Child Opportunity Index (COI) is an external benchmark layer used for comparison. It is not part of the YOI calculation.

Link: diversitydatakids.org/child-opportunity-index
How it is used
The dashboard loads a processed San Diego–only COI file and displays it as a comparison overlay. This allows users to visually compare YOI patterns against an external child-opportunity benchmark.

Workforce report validation context

The dashboard includes a validation/context layer based on the San Diego Youth Workforce report. This is used to check whether the YOI dashboard tells a similar regional story, especially around youth population, education, employment, and service access.

Countywide context indicators
The dashboard currently includes countywide context indicators for CDE graduation rate, CDE dropout rate, homeless students, homeless student rate, English learner students, ACS PUMS youth unemployment ages 16–19, and ACS PUMS labor-force participation ages 16–19.
Why they are countywide
These values come from sources that are not all tract-level. CDE graduation, dropout, homelessness, and English learner data are school/county-based. PUMS unemployment is estimated at PUMA/county scale. Therefore, these values are shown as countywide context and should not be interpreted as selected-tract values.
Current benchmark values
Current countywide validation values include: graduation rate 84.6%, dropout rate 7.9%, homeless students 23,641, homeless student rate 4.7%, English learner students 74,812, youth unemployment ages 16–19 at 18.8%, and labor-force participation ages 16–19 at 33.0%.
Validation interpretation
Validation is directional, not exact. The dashboard matches strongly on regional youth population scale, countywide education outcomes, South San Diego economic distress, and Metro San Diego’s role as a major youth/service hub. It only partially matches the report on North County and East San Diego because the YOI uses broader opportunity domains than the Workforce report’s specific education and labor-market metrics.

Current validation summary

Strong matches
The dashboard strongly matches the Workforce report on regional youth population scale, countywide graduation/dropout context, South San Diego’s economic distress, and Metro San Diego’s role as the largest youth/service hub.
Partial matches
The dashboard only partially matches the Workforce report on North County’s economic strength and East San Diego’s education weakness. North County appears weaker in the current YOI because of low Youth Supports and Education scores, while the Workforce report emphasizes North County’s stronger labor-force attachment.
Main conclusion
The YOI dashboard supports directional alignment with the Workforce report, but it should not be described as an exact replication. It is best understood as a complementary multi-domain spatial tool.

Known limitations

Different geographies
Not every source is available at the same geography. Some indicators are tract-level, some are school or county-level, and some are PUMA-level. This limits exact one-to-one comparison across all metrics.
Different years
The dashboard currently combines the latest available processed sources, which may not all come from the same year. Workforce report comparisons should therefore be interpreted carefully when years differ.
Service taxonomy
Service classifications are still approximate. Some services may fit more than one category, and the dashboard taxonomy may not exactly match the Workforce report taxonomy.
Indicator placeholders
Some ACS-based indicators are currently proxy measures or heuristic column selections. These should be audited before the index is treated as final or used for formal policy evaluation.
Index interpretation
The YOI is a relative opportunity score within San Diego County. A score does not measure program effectiveness, individual youth outcomes, or causal impact.

Next methodology steps

1. Workforce-aligned regional metrics
Add exact Metro/North/South/East values for youth unemployment, labor-force participation, graduation, dropout, postsecondary enrollment, credential attainment, and youth segment counts.
2. Service taxonomy audit
Review service classifications, especially employment-related and transition-pathway services, so the dashboard categories better match the Workforce report and stakeholder expectations.
3. Education domain refinement
Keep the broad YOI Education domain, but document which indicators belong in it and maintain the separate Workforce-aligned education benchmark for Figure 10 validation.
4. Full youth segment integration
Add report-style regional fields for students, not-in-school youth, English learners, youth in low-income households, refugees, youth experiencing homelessness, foster youth, and other priority youth segments.
5. Data dictionary and source audit
Produce a final data dictionary listing each indicator, source, year, geography, directionality, transformation, and domain assignment.
