# Workforce Report Validation Summary

The Youth Opportunity Index dashboard was validated against the Workforce report using a combination of countywide context indicators and county-region YOI patterns. Because the Workforce report combines multiple geographies and data sources, including CDE school/county data, ACS/PUMS labor-force estimates, and regional analysis, the validation should be interpreted as directional rather than an exact one-to-one numeric replication.

At the countywide level, the dashboard now includes several Workforce-relevant benchmark indicators. These include a CDE adjusted cohort graduation rate of 84.6%, a four-year cohort dropout rate of 7.9%, 23,641 homeless students, a homeless student rate of 4.7%, 74,812 English learner students, an ACS PUMS youth unemployment rate of 18.8% for ages 16–19, and a labor force participation rate of 33.0% for ages 16–19. These values provide countywide context for the dashboard and should not be interpreted as selected-tract or selected-district values.

At the county-region level, the dashboard shows partial but meaningful alignment with the Workforce report. The strongest alignment appears in the economic domain: South San Diego has the lowest economic score among the four county regions, with an economic score of 38.0/100. This supports the Workforce report’s emphasis on labor-market distress in South San Diego. East San Diego also shows low overall opportunity, with the second-lowest overall YOI score at 44.6/100, and its weakest domain is Health at 34.0/100. Metro San Diego has the highest overall YOI score at 51.7/100 and the strongest youth-supports score, suggesting that it functions more as a service and opportunity hub than as the region with the most severe overall opportunity deficit.

However, the dashboard does not perfectly reproduce the Workforce report’s regional narrative. North County appears as the lowest overall YOI region in the dashboard, with an overall score of 44.3/100. This result is driven especially by a very low Youth Supports score of 28.2/100 and weaker Education score of 42.4/100. This suggests that the dashboard is identifying additional service-access or youth-support gaps that may not be the central emphasis of the Workforce report’s labor-market framing.

Overall, the validation supports the dashboard as a complementary regional opportunity tool. It aligns most clearly with the Workforce report on South San Diego’s economic distress and broader uneven opportunity across East and inland communities. At the same time, differences in North County show that the YOI dashboard should not be treated as a direct replica of the Workforce report. Instead, it should be used to compare regional opportunity patterns, identify additional areas of concern, and provide a multi-domain view of youth opportunity across San Diego County.

## Limitation

This validation is directional. CDE graduation, dropout, homelessness, and English learner indicators are school/county-based. ACS PUMS youth unemployment is estimated at PUMA/county scale. The YOI dashboard is built from tract, ZIP, district, and county-region aggregations. Because these sources do not share the same geography, validation supports regional pattern consistency rather than exact tract-level agreement.
